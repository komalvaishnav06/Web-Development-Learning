About india

The Indian subcontinent, the great landmass of South Asia, is the home of one of the world’s oldest and most influential civilizations. In this article, the subcontinent, which for historical purposes is usually called simply “India,” is understood to comprise the areas of not only the present-day Republic of India (free from British rule since August 15, 1947, celebrated as the country’s Independence Day) but also the republics of Pakistan (partitioned from India in 1947) and Bangladesh (which formed the eastern part of Pakistan until its independence in 1971). For the histories of these latter two countries since their creation, see Pakistan and Bangladesh.

Ancient India

Ancient India developed some of the world’s earliest cities, influential systems of government, major religious traditions, and enduring intellectual achievements. Its history stretches from the urban settlements of the Indus Valley Civilization, which flourished more than 4,000 years ago, through powerful empires such as the Maurya and Gupta dynasties. Across these centuries, people in the Indian subcontinent developed sophisticated approaches to agriculture, trade, architecture, mathematics, medicine, philosophy, and literature. These achievements emerged through interactions among diverse communities, changing political systems, environmental conditions, and networks connecting India with neighboring regions. Understanding ancient India requires looking beyond individual rulers and monuments. Its history was shaped by the relationship between geography and settlement, the growth and decline of cities, the movement of goods and ideas, and the development of cultural traditions that changed over time. Although some aspects of this history remain uncertain, archaeological discoveries and surviving texts reveal a complex civilization whose influence extended far beyond the subcontinent.
